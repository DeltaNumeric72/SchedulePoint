D. WHY THE DRAIN CAN NEVER TAKE IT — two independent reasons

  1. `crash-restart.test.ts`'s drainer is started WITHOUT a signer, so
     `startOutboxRunner` registers `outbox.dispatch` and NOTHING else
     (`apps/api/src/outbox/runner.ts`: `periodicTasks` is `{}` when
     `options.signer === undefined`). graphile-worker only claims jobs whose task
     identifier is in the task list. An `audit.checkpoint` job is invisible to it
     forever — no timeout, no error, no retry. Just a count that never reaches 0.

  2. The row is LOCKED by a pool that no longer exists, and the drainer passes
     `staleAfterMs: 3_600_000` precisely so that it will not reclaim anybody's
     lease. Even a drainer that knew the task could not have it for an hour.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
E. WHERE THE JOB COMES FROM — `apps/api/test/audit/periodic.test.ts`

Both `audit.checkpoint` rows in the poll are its. The poll shows the sequence:

    10:45:42  job 1351 enqueued, attempts 0, available   (never runs — by design)
    10:45:45  job 1352 enqueued, claimed by pool-ff544f08fcafabf3ec, attempts 1
    10:45:55  job 1351 gone; job 1352 still locked, and stays locked to the end

1351 is the job the test `a runner without a signer registers NO periodic task,
loudly rather than silently` enqueues on purpose to prove nothing executes it.
That test deletes every `audit.%` job in its `finally` — which is where 1351
goes at ~10:45:5x.

1352 is the job `enqueuing audit.checkpoint by name makes the real runner write a
checkpoint` (R-03) enqueues; its own signer-bearing runner claims it
(attempts→1). The test then polls `checkpointCount` until it increments and
returns, and `finally { await runner.stop() }` stops the runner. The checkpoint
row commits inside the task; the job's COMPLETION is written by graphile-worker
after the task resolves. The poll shows a run in which the completion did not
happen: the row stays `attempts=1, locked_at set, locked_by` a dead pool.

Two conditions have to line up, and both are order- and race-dependent, which is
exactly the observed 3-to-4-in-7 flakiness:

    (i)  `--sequence.shuffle.tests` puts the no-signer test FIRST, so its
         `delete from graphile_worker._private_jobs where task_id in (... 'audit.%')`
         runs BEFORE R-03 enqueues 1352 and therefore cleans up nothing of R-03's;
    (ii) R-03's `runner.stop()` races its own in-flight job and leaves it locked.

Either one alone is survivable. Together they hand a permanently undrainable job
to whatever file next tries to empty the queue.
